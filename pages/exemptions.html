Exemptions

The following categories are currently automatically exempt:

  • Non-immigrant visa holders such as foreign students, visitors, tourists, and diplomats are exempt.
  • Active-duty military personnel are exempt from the draft.
  • Women are currently exempt from the selective service draft.

The following individuals are also exempt from military induction but must still register until December 2026.

To claim the following exemptions simply send the Selective Service a LETTER OF EXEMPTION CLAIM to your selective service "local board" postmarked within ten days of receiving a draft notice in the mail. Your letter informs the selective service that you are claiming your exemptions and you are not required to show up at MEPS for processing — your letter will be reviewed by the local volunteer claims board and the local volunteer board will schedule your exemption meeting for approval or disapproval.

The following categories are entitled to exemption and or deferment:

  • Duly ordained ministers, Clergy / regular ministers (not ordained), ministerial students
  • Men with disabilities —
  • Undocumented immigrants
  • Sole surviving sons —

Deferments if drafted

  • High school students — until graduation or age 20
  • College students — until end of current semester
  • Hardship — induction would cause severe hardship to dependent Children
  • Disqualifying medical or psychological conditions (determined at MEPS)
  • Conscientious objector status (moral/religious opposition to war) — sub categories of conscientious objector:
  1. Member of a church with conscientious objector theology
  2. Self held beliefs
  3. You are the chief duly ordained minister of a church entity with conscience objector tenets. You maintain, teach, preach, and/or evangelize faith based conscientious objector canon- theology.
  • Certain elected officials — while in office
  • Veterans who already served

Important foot note - Local boards currently exist only in "standby" status — they aren't actively staffed or operating unless a draft is reactivated, and Selective Service doesn't publish local board addresses or contact information. So in practice, there's often nowhere to mail a letter to.

The practical solution: since MEPS's entire purpose is to determine your "availability," you can drop your exemption claim letter off at the MEPS front desk in person — get a receipt or signed proof of delivery — or simply mail it to your local MEPS address, which, unlike the standby local boards, is active and publicly listed.

Process: A claim is filed or mailed → local board reviews and decides → if granted, exempt, no further testing needed (since availability-for-induction is already resolved) → if entitlement is denied, registrant may appeal multiple times → only once appeals are exhausted does the registrant proceed toward MEPS evaluation.

Footnote: You are entitled by law to show up at MEPS and claim your exemptions in person — that is the sole purpose MEPS exists to serve. The letter option isn't a requirement; it's an accommodation, a legal courtesy that lets you avoid making the trip to a MEPS processing station when your exemption can already be established on paper, saving you (and specifically MEPS) the time of an in-person process that would reach the same result. Such that if you did show up to a MEPS station on the date of the draft letter notice MEPS would send you home anyhow but MEPS would have wasted precious time that could have instead been used to process other applicants.

Best Strategy: The clearest way to proceed is simple: make your claim at MEPS. Be courteous — don't waste MEPS staff's time. Make your exemption claim immediately upon arrival so they can forward your case to the local board without further processing and send you home immediately!

This is simply how the process works, step by step. The draftee fulfills his "show up" obligation the first time by reporting to MEPS on the scheduled date and making his exemption claim in person (32 C.F.R. § 1624.5(a) sets that date at least 10 days after the order is issued). MEPS then forwards the exemption claim to a volunteer local board (32 C.F.R. § 1605.53), who are required to give at least 10 days' notice before the appearance (32 C.F.R. § 1648.4(a)). The draftee actually fulfills his legal liability to "show up" obligation a second time by appearing at that board hearing. That's at least 20 days built into the process before the case is even heard the first time — and since 10 days is only the required minimum, not a maximum, the actual hearing date depends on the board's own schedule and could land further out.

If a scheduled date doesn't work, the draftee can "push" the date out further under law (32 C.F.R. § 1648.4(b)). The volunteer board must then find time to review the request and respond, and the draftee can respond in turn — each exchange adding further time before the case is finally heard. The local board's decision on your "AVAILABILTY" for induction can then be appealed to the district appeal board, then to the National Appeal Board, and finally to the "real" court system. This all takes time!
