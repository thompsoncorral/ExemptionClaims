ASVAB

Score 9th percentile or below on the AFQT, and you're disqualified from military service — no exemption needed.

Many draftees will be denied induction into military service because they achieve a failing score on the ASVAB. If you score in Category V (1st–9th percentile) on the AFQT, you cannot join the military.

"Exemption = a status-based legal carve-out. It exists regardless of your physical condition — it's about who you are (a duly ordained minister, or a conscientious objector). An exemption is something you claim, and a board grants it based on your status/beliefs, not your body.

  • Disqualification = a fitness-based finding. It's about whether your body/mind meets the military's medical, mental, and moral standards. This is what MEPS actually exists to determine. You don't "claim" a disqualification — MEPS tests for it and either finds you fit or unfit.

So where does the ASVAB fit? The ASVAB (Armed Services Vocational Aptitude Battery) is neither an exemption nor strictly a medical disqualification — it's a third, separate category: an aptitude/qualification screen. §3803(a) itself references this directly — it sets "the passing requirement for the Armed Forces Qualification Test... at a percentile score of 10 points." Scoring below that threshold doesn't exempt you (you're not claiming any status) and it isn't a medical disqualification (nothing physically or mentally wrong necessarily) — it's a failure to meet the minimum aptitude standard, which independently bars induction."
