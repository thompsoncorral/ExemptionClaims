Surprise! No More College Deferment!

What most people call the "college deferment" no longer exists. Before 1971, full-time students got Class II-S — protection that ran until graduation, effectively covering the whole college degree program of the student! A college student was historically deferred from the draft lottery, but not anymore.

Congress ended that in 1971 (via H.R. 6531, signed by Nixon). What replaced it isn't a college deferment at all — it's a semester deferment. Under current Selective Service rules, a student's induction can only be postponed to the end of the current semester (or, for seniors, the end of the academic year) — not until the degree is finished.

That means, for example, if a draftee receives a draft notice in the middle of a school semester — say, around Thanksgiving — the draftee would now be forced to process at MEPS a few weeks later in mid-December at the end of the school semester, regardless of how much of the degree remains. The government still calls it "college deferment," but Americans will be shocked to learn it's only a few weeks' deferment. I guess if you get the draft notice on the first day of college classes, then you get a few months before they take you to MEPS!

So college and university is definitely not a recommended entitlement program. United States, 346 U.S. 389, 393–94 — simultaneous secular work permitted). One can be a full time preacher and student, or full time preacher and full time employee, at the same time — then you could stay in school as long as you needed to complete your degree while evangelizing your college peers for faith based purposes and missions of your church!
