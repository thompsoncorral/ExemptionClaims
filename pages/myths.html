Myths

People believe the false MYTH that men are "drafted into the military," but in reality a draft notice only requires men to report or "show up" at MEPS for evaluation. (50 U.S.C. § 3803)

What a draftee should know

You cannot be directly drafted into the military. A draft letter is simply an order telling you to show up at MEPS for medical, mental, and exemption evaluation.

Registration is not induction — what the statute actually says

Under 50 U.S.C. § 3803(a), every registrant shall be "immediately liable for classification and examination, and shall, as soon as practicable following his registration, be so classified and examined, both physically and mentally, in order to determine his AVAILABILITY for induction for military training and service in the Armed Forces." Induction is explicitly a separate, determination — induction is not the same event as being called up for evaluation by a draft letter. Showing up for evaluation is what the law requires. What happens afterward is a different legal question.

After December 15, 2026 all young men are no longer liable for "classification" because the selective service will "automatically register" men for the draft process. Therefor all draftees only have one lawful obligation or liability - to show up at MEPS for exemption entitlements and medical examinations - additionally a draftee can send a exemption letter ten days prior to any MEPS evaluation appointment date to defer that evaluation and shift the entire process to the local volunteer appeals board.

YOU ARE NOT LIABLE TO JOIN THE MILITARY - you are liable to "show up" to MEPS for an examination of "availability". Additionally a draftee can send meps an exemption letter ten days before your MEPS evaluation date so the selective service appeals board can "determine your exemptions to availability". That's it nothing more! So if you "show up" to examination and verbally claim exemptions at MEPS or you send your official exemption letter up to ten days ahead of time you have fulfilled all your lawfull obligations of the entire selective service process and complied with the law in totality!

How a draft would actually be processed, if one were ever activated

  • Lottery: Each of the 365 birth dates is randomly assigned a number, 1–365, fixing everyone's position in the sequence.
  • Age-group priority: Age 20 is called first, then 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, then back to 19, then 18 year old males.
  • Local boards send draft notices to names that match the random lottery birthdates chosen by the selective service lottery system.

mail is sent to young men with birthdates that match the random lottery drawing numbers and ordered to report for "classification" (notice how you are ordered to report for "classification" - you are not ordered to join the military!) You are ordered to basically have a medical exam for physical/mental/and moral evaluation at MEPS and to claim any or all exemptions at MEPS or agin via the letter you send them prior to an exam.

  • A draftees exemption claims are filed at this stage — you can send MEPS a letter ten days prior to your scheduled examination date or you can simply show up at MEPS, waste their time and inform Meps what lawful exemptions you will be claiming. Since the purposes of the classification exam is to litarly determine exemptions a draftee can absolutely inform MEPS of his exemption claims at the MEPS facility and any time during the "classification process" in layman's terms if you get drafted you can always simply follow the law and "show up" at MEPS according to the draft notice and tell MEPS that you are exempt.
  • Your exemption claim will be sent to the local volunteer board of appeal. Your exemption claims are exactly what is transferred to the local volunteer board of appeals. MEPS cannot attempt to perform any evaluations of any kind if you have submitted verbally or in writing an exemption claims. A pending appeal is a distinct status — a draftee is neither inducted, deferred, nor exempted until the all levels of appeals are resolved. An exemption claim puts all MEPS evaluations on permanent hold until the entire appeals process is complete to include appealing to a "real" judicial court system.
